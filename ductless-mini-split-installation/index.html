Think about the spaces your central system has never handled well: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs hot all summer, or an older Draper home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the spaces a ductless mini split was made for. There is no ductwork to leak or lose energy, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor modulates to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it runs as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Good ductless comfort starts with a load calculation, not a catalog. We size each zone to what that room actually needs, since an oversized head short-cycles, leaves the air clammy, and never quite holds temperature. With multiple zones, the engineering gets stricter: capacity has to be split sensibly, and line set length and elevation change must stay inside the manufacturer's tables or the system quietly loses performance. Style matters too, because a wall head, a ceiling cassette, and a low floor unit each change both airflow and the look of the room. Our experienced technicians walk through the space with you, agree on placements that work physically and visually, and plan line set routes that stay hidden where possible and neat where they show.

On installation day, the details that customers never see are the ones we refuse to skip. Every flare is formed carefully, torqued to spec, and leak-checked, because a slowly weeping flare is the number one way mini splits fail. The line set gets a nitrogen pressure test, then a deep vacuum confirmed on a micron gauge, before refrigerant ever moves. We set up condensate to drain by gravity or through a reliable pump, trim the charge for the actual run length, and test each zone in heating and cooling. The Benefits of Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation

Most failed mini splits we see in Draper come from installs that treated the job as simple. Refrigerant leaks at the flares are the headline problem: a joint that was poorly formed or never torqued to spec leaks slowly, and the homeowner finds out a season later when the head stops cooling. Moisture is the next issue, because an install that skips the nitrogen pressure test and a micron-verified vacuum leaves water vapor inside a system whose inverter compressor is sensitive to it. Overlong or overly tall line set runs sap capacity quietly, and a drain line without enough fall will drip down the wall as soon as humidity climbs.

Here is the part most installers underestimate: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Draper HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and commissioning each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a weekend project. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of a rushed install that never quite works.

How to Prevent Ductless Mini Split Installation Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
  • Get a yearly professional checkup of charge, flare joints, and drainage while any fix is still small
  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
