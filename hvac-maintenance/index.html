Warning Signs for HVAC Maintenance

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • New noises when the equipment starts up
  • You simply have no idea what condition your equipment is actually in
  • Energy bills creeping up without any change in your habits
  • A failure last year that seemed to come out of nowhere
  • A newer system whose warranty terms expect documented maintenance
  • Longer run times to get to the thermostat setting
  • Your last professional tune-up was more than twelve months ago
  • Filters you cannot remember changing, or a musty smell when the blower starts

The Benefits of Professional HVAC Maintenance

There is a short list of things homeowners can handle: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and glancing at the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded look perfectly normal. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks the same as a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws creeping toward the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix the season before they fail.

Gas heating adds a safety dimension that goes beyond cost. Burners, flame proving, venting, and draft need checking with instruments every year, because when they fail, the result can be combustion gases where they do not belong, not just a chilly house. That is why our experienced technicians treat safety testing as the foundation of every fall visit rather than an add-on. Over time, Draper HVAC builds a history of your readings, and the trend lines show which parts are aging and which still have seasons left. Your money goes only to what is actually drifting, replaced when it suits you, at a planned price rather than an emergency one.

How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Pay attention to new noises or longer cycles and mention them at your next visit
  • Schedule maintenance in early spring and early fall, ahead of the busy season and the extreme weather
  • Resist closing vents in spare rooms; the system is designed with all registers open
  • Replace your filter on a one- to three-month cycle; nothing else you do pays off more
  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Flush the condensate line every spring so algae cannot build up and clog it
  • Check carbon monoxide alarms monthly in any home with fuel-burning appliances
