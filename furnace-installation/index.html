Size matters more than most people expect with a new furnace. An oversized unit short-cycles, overshoots the thermostat, and wears out early, while a correctly sized furnace runs longer and more quietly and costs less to operate. So Draper HVAC starts every replacement in Draper with a load calculation, not the number on the old nameplate. From there, the choices come into focus: 80 percent AFUE or a condensing furnace in the mid-90s, single-stage, two-stage, or modulating burners, and a basic PSC blower or a variable-speed ECM motor that saves electricity and smooths out temperature swings. The right combination depends on your home, your ducts, and how long your heating season lasts, and you will be living with it for the next twenty years.

Most of a furnace's lifespan is decided on installation day. Condensing models produce acidic condensate that has to be trapped, sloped, and drained properly, and their PVC intake and exhaust pipes must be sized, pitched, and terminated exactly as the manufacturer specifies. Get those details wrong and you can expect nuisance lockouts, or worse, flue gas where it does not belong. The gas line needs a sediment trap and a leak-tested connection. The ductwork has to deliver the airflow the furnace was designed for, so our experienced technicians take static pressure readings instead of assuming, and fix restrictions during the changeout when the readings call for it. We also handle permits wherever they apply.

The last hour of the job matters as much as the first. Our technicians run a combustion analysis, set gas pressure to spec, check temperature rise against the data plate and tune the blower to it, confirm the vent is drafting, and watch the furnace through complete cycles. That process is called commissioning, and it is standard on every Draper HVAC install. We then show you how the thermostat works, where the filter goes, and what normal looks like for the first season. Warning Signs for Furnace Installation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Rust, soot, or loud ignition that returns even after repairs
  • Your existing furnace has passed fifteen to twenty years of service
  • Service calls that come more often and cost more every year
  • Paying more to heat a house that never gets comfortable
  • A cracked heat exchanger has been found, ending the repair conversation
  • Short cycling, big temperature swings, or rooms the furnace never reaches
  • A new air conditioner or heat pump is going in and the old furnace cannot keep up
  • Fan noise loud enough to interrupt conversation or sleep

Why Hire a Professional for Furnace Installation?

What separates a good furnace installation from a bad one is mostly invisible in the finished product. The venting on a condensing unit has to match the manufacturer's tables for size, length, slope, and termination, or it will trip pressure faults and can put flue gases where they should never be. Gas lines need correct sizing, a sediment trap, and an instrument leak test. Condensate drains need real slope, because the acidic water keeps coming all winter and a flat line ends up as a midwinter lockout or a leak into the framing. Because none of this is visible in a finished photo, it is where shortcuts happen.

Just as important are sizing and commissioning. A furnace chosen by rule of thumb is usually too big, and an oversized furnace short-cycles its way through a shorter, harder life while delivering worse comfort. Draper HVAC sizes from a real load calculation instead. After installation, our technicians commission the system with instruments: combustion analysis, gas pressure adjustment, temperature rise measured and held in the rated band by adjusting the blower, and a static pressure check to confirm the ductwork suits the furnace. It is the last hour of the job that turns new equipment into two decades of quiet, dependable heat.

How to Prevent Furnace Installation Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have the furnace inspected each autumn to keep burners tuned and readings on target
  • Keep paints, solvents, and chlorine cleaners out of the furnace area
  • Keep the installation documents and model numbers handy for fast, accurate future service
  • Clear leaves, drifted snow, and nests away from the intake and exhaust pipes
  • Replace filters on time from day one to keep air moving freely through the new system
  • On condensing furnaces, check that the condensate drain flows freely before cold weather
  • Avoid deep temperature setbacks, since a right-sized furnace works best with long, steady cycles
